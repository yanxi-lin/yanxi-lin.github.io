About the research

Neural networks deployed in energy- and safety-critical applications need arithmetic that is both efficient and resilient. DPAM is a fault-tolerant approximate multiplier based on the Mitchell logarithmic principle.

The design protects its two arithmetic paths differently: approximate triple modular redundancy in the exponent path, and a truncated self-detecting adder in the mantissa path. This hybrid approach targets lower hardware overhead while maintaining fault tolerance.

Evaluation combines hardware measurements, gate-level fault injection, and neural network workloads to study the trade-off between efficiency and reliability.
